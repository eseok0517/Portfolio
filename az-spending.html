Arizona public spending

A review of federal contract awards performed in Arizona. It shows where the money goes by agency, industry and vendor, measures how concentrated it is, and lists awards whose records need follow-up, such as expired contracts with unspent balances. It applies the same purchasing-controls approach as my earlier Seattle and California work to one region and one consistent source.

Method and limits

  • Source. USAspending.gov, the federal government’s public spending database. Prime contract awards (types A to D) with place of performance in Arizona and an award amount of at least $100,000, for the last three federal fiscal years. A weekly GitHub Action refreshes the file.
  • Coverage. The data API returns at most 10,000 records per query, so each fiscal year holds that year’s largest awards. Awards that appear in more than one year are counted once. Smaller awards are not included. Grants, loans and subcontracts are not included.
  • Concentration. Top-1, top-5 and top-10 vendor share of award dollars, and the Herfindahl-Hirschman Index (HHI). Antitrust agencies commonly read an HHI under 1,500 as unconcentrated, 1,500 to 2,500 as moderate and above 2,500 as high. The index is used here only as a yardstick for vendor concentration, not a market-power finding.
  • Review lists. Expired with unspent balance: end date passed and at least $100,000 of the award not yet paid, a candidate for closeout or deobligation. Paid above award amount: outlays exceed the award by more than $1,000. Invalid dates: end date before start date.
  • Limits. Award amounts are obligations, not final cost. Outlays can lag, and agencies report on different schedules. Recipient names are as reported, so one company can appear under variants and understate its concentration.
