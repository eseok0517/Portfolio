Banking · Arizona · Call-report analysis

Arizona bank health monitor

A quarterly scorecard for banks headquartered in Arizona, measured against the ten largest U.S. banks. It tracks profitability, net interest margin, capital, liquidity and deposit trends from FDIC call reports, and flags banks that cross simple warning thresholds, the way a credit or bank analyst would screen a peer group.

Findings (Q2 2026 call reports)

  • Arizona banks earn about half what the largest banks do. Median return on assets was 0.58% against 1.27% for the ten largest U.S. banks, though it is up from 0.33% a year earlier.
  • They lend out much more of their deposits. Median loans were 86% of deposits against 60% at the largest banks, and net interest margin was higher (3.77% vs 2.95%), but the median efficiency ratio was 79% vs 54%, so more of each revenue dollar goes to costs.
  • Nine of the twelve banks tripped at least one warning screen. High loans-to-deposits (6 banks) and low ROA (5) were the most common; two of the ten largest banks tripped one (thin equity).
  • Two banks hold almost all of the assets. USAA Federal Savings Bank and Western Alliance together hold about $205 billion of the $208 billion in Arizona-headquartered bank assets, so the other ten behave like community banks.

Method and limits

  • Source. FDIC BankFind API, quarterly call reports. Arizona banks are active institutions with an Arizona headquarters. The comparison group is the ten largest active U.S. banks by assets at the time of the last refresh. A weekly GitHub Action updates the file.
  • Measures. Return on assets, net interest margin and efficiency ratio are as reported by the FDIC. Equity / assets and loans / deposits are computed from reported balances. Deposit growth compares each quarter with the same quarter a year earlier.
  • Limits. Many Arizona-headquartered institutions are small, so single-quarter figures can swing. Bank holding companies and bank subsidiaries can differ, and this analysis uses the bank-level filing. National banks that operate heavily in Arizona but are headquartered elsewhere appear only if they are among the ten largest. Regulatory capital ratios are not shown because not every institution reports every field.
